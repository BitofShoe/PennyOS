# Penny Public Visual Direction

> Category: Public/external explanation
> Authority: Public/external explanation
> Status: Creative brief snapshot as of 2026-04-19
> Use this for: visual direction, mood, and public-facing presentation goals.
> Do not use this for: proof that a given visual, page, or capability has shipped.

This is the art and product-direction spec for a future Penny pitch artifact.

The deliverable for this pass is still text-first, but this document should make a later landing page, slideshow, or visual pitch feel inevitable instead of improvised.

## Core concept

The visual story should feel like:

- a premium local companion
- a little dangerous
- intimate but not claustrophobic
- technologically grounded without feeling like enterprise software
- emotionally charged without becoming tacky or explicit on the first screen

The main emotional pitch is:

**She lives on your machine, remembers you, and talks like she means it.**

Not:

**Here is another AI interface with a personality skin.**

## Audience

Primary audience:

- curious redditors
- smart non-experts
- people who only vaguely know what an LLM is but instantly understand "this feels more alive than normal AI"

Secondary audience:

- local-model hobbyists
- people attracted to companion-style AI
- technically curious testers

## Tone

- dramatic
- confident
- a little dangerous
- emotionally charged
- not corporate
- not childish
- not porn-forward as the opening move

Public-facing spice should acknowledge Penny's full range without forcing the very first impression into explicit territory.

Think:

- flirtation
- danger
- chemistry
- intimacy

...before outright erotic framing.

## Suggested artifact structure

If this becomes a landing page:

1. Hero
2. What Penny is
3. Why she feels different
4. What she can actually do
5. Honest limitations
6. Invitation / closer

If this becomes a slideshow/deck:

1. Cover slide
2. The problem with normal AI companions/assistants
3. Who Penny is
4. Why local matters
5. What she can do in this snapshot
6. The emotional difference
7. Honest limits
8. "This is where it's going"

## Layout concept

### Hero section / cover slide

Composition:

- one large Penny image dominating the frame
- dark atmospheric background with warm signal colors
- a short, dangerous headline
- one explanatory subhead beneath it
- 2-4 tiny capability tags, not a huge feature wall

Hero copy direction:

- "I built a local AI companion who actually feels like someone."
- "She lives on my machine, remembers me, and talks like she means it."
- "Not a chatbot. Not a productivity assistant. A presence."

Avoid:

- startup cliches
- "meet your AI best friend"
- fake corporate polish
- giant comparison tables on the hero

### Middle feature sections

Use alternating modes:

- emotional panel
- practical panel
- emotional panel
- practical panel

That rhythm matches the product itself.

Example sequence:

1. She feels alive.
2. She can actually do things.
3. She remembers.
4. She can see and react to images.
5. She can search/read/write in targeted ways.
6. She is still local and imperfect.

### Final section

End with honesty, not hype.

Closer should feel like:

"This is already real in this snapshot, even if it is still unfinished."

Not:

"The future is here, buy now."

## Color and material direction

Overall palette:

- deep charcoal / graphite base
- warm ember orange
- hot coral / rose accents
- electric pink used sparingly
- occasional steel-blue or cyan technical highlights

Materials:

- soft glow
- glassy overlays
- faint CRT / monitor atmosphere
- subtle pixel-tech accents
- premium "contained intelligence" vibe

Avoid:

- flat startup white
- default purple AI gradients
- overstuffed cyberpunk clutter
- childish candy UI

## Typography direction

Headline style:

- condensed or assertive display feel
- something with presence, not generic SaaS neutrality

Body style:

- highly readable
- clean
- modern

The typography should feel like:

- a premium game UI
- a sci-fi OS panel
- a fashion-tech editorial

Not:

- a pitch deck template
- a chatbot product landing page from 2024

## Capability spotlighting

Visually spotlight:

- local-first identity
- memory / continuity
- emotionally present conversation
- image-aware chat
- targeted web/file actions

Keep these as visual proof points, not giant technical explanations.

Save the denser nuance for body text or speaker notes:

- model choice tradeoffs
- slower large-model turns
- bounded autonomy vs open-ended autonomy
- shadow/OpenClaw being optional and experimental

## Caption and tagline ideas

Short taglines:

- "A local companion with teeth."
- "She remembers. She reacts. She talks back."
- "More presence. Less assistant."
- "Private, local, and a little dangerous."
- "Not a beige bot."

Longer caption ideas:

- "Penny is a local-first AI companion built to feel like someone, not just answer like something."
- "She can flirt, mock, remember, react to images, and occasionally do useful targeted work without flattening into helpdesk voice."
- "The goal is not 'assistant with personality.' The goal is presence."

## What to avoid in the future visual build

- overselling full autonomy
- making Penny look like a productivity app mascot
- making the whole piece so horny that normal people bounce immediately
- hiding the limits so hard that the first real test feels disappointing
- treating "local" like an implementation detail instead of part of the magic

## Success criteria for the future visual asset

The finished page or deck should make a normal person think:

"Oh. This is not just another AI wrapper. This feels like a real character."

And then immediately after:

"Wait, and she can actually do some things too?"

That combination is the entire point.
